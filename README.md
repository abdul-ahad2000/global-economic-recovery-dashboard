# Impact of COVID-19 on the Global Economy (2020–2023)

## Project Overview

The COVID-19 pandemic significantly disrupted the global economy, affecting employment, economic growth, inflation, monetary policy and financial markets. Lockdowns, business closures and supply chain disruptions created economic challenges across countries, while governments and central banks introduced various measures to support recovery.

This project explores the economic impact of COVID-19 across nine countries and economic regions using macroeconomic indicators and financial market data. The analysis examines how different economies responded to the pandemic and how their economic conditions evolved during the recovery period.

The objective is to identify differences in economic performance, understand recovery patterns and highlight important factors influencing global economic stability.

## Research Objectives

The analysis focuses on six key research questions:

1. **Unemployment:** Which countries experienced the most severe unemployment during the pandemic, and had employment levels recovered by 2023?
2. **Monetary Policy:** How did central banks adjust interest rates during and after the COVID-19 pandemic?
3. **GDP Growth:** Which economies experienced the greatest GDP contraction, and which demonstrated the strongest recovery?
4. **Economic Activity:** How did manufacturing, services and consumer confidence relate to economic growth?
5. **Inflation:** Was there a relationship between rising inflation and slower GDP growth during and after the pandemic?
6. **Financial Markets:** Which periods experienced the strongest upward trends and highest volatility in financial market values?

## Key Findings

### 1. Unemployment Trends

The United States experienced one of the sharpest unemployment increases during the initial stages of the pandemic.

- US unemployment reached approximately 14.9% in April 2020.
- Canada's unemployment reached approximately 13.7% during the same period.
- By the end of 2023, US unemployment had declined to approximately 3.9%.
- Most of the economies demonstrated improvements in employment conditions by 2023.

The findings highlight differences in labour market resilience and the speed of economic recovery across countries.

### 2. Changes in Interest Rates

Central banks introduced significant monetary policy adjustments in response to the pandemic.

- Interest rates were reduced to support economic activity during the initial crisis.
- The US interest rate was approximately 0.25% in 2021.
- By 2023, the US interest rate had increased to approximately 5.15%.
- Several other economies, including New Zealand, Canada, the United Kingdom and Australia, also increased interest rates to address inflation.

This demonstrates the transition from economic stimulus measures towards tighter monetary policies.

### 3. GDP Contraction and Recovery

The pandemic caused substantial economic contractions across major economies in 2020.

Some of the notable GDP figures included:

| Economy | GDP Growth in 2020 |
|---|---:|
| United Kingdom | -10.35% |
| Europe | -6.23% |
| Canada | -5.00% |
| United States | -2.23% |
| China | 8.23% |

China was the only economy in this comparison to maintain positive GDP growth in 2020.

By 2023, the United States demonstrated stronger economic growth compared with several other developed economies, including the United Kingdom and Canada.

### 4. Manufacturing, Services and Consumer Confidence

Manufacturing PMI, Services PMI and Consumer Confidence provide additional insights into economic performance.

The analysis highlights that improvements in manufacturing and services activity do not necessarily translate into stronger economic growth.

Consumer confidence also plays an important role in economic recovery, as consumer spending influences demand, business activity and overall economic performance.

### 5. Inflation and GDP Growth

Inflation became a significant economic challenge during the post-pandemic recovery.

Supply chain disruptions, energy price increases and changes in consumer demand contributed to rising inflation across several economies.

The analysis compares inflation and GDP growth to investigate their relationship.

An important observation is that lower inflation does not automatically translate into stronger GDP growth. Switzerland, for example, experienced relatively low inflation alongside comparatively modest economic growth.

### 6. Financial Market Trends

Financial markets experienced considerable uncertainty during the pandemic.

- March 2020 marked a period of significant financial market declines.
- Markets generally experienced an upward trend during the subsequent recovery.
- The period from mid-2022 to 2023 showed increased volatility.
- Inflation concerns, geopolitical developments and interest rate increases contributed to changing investor sentiment.

The analysis examines financial market movements to understand how investor confidence evolved throughout the pandemic and recovery period.

## Recommendations

Based on the findings, several areas deserve attention when preparing economies for future disruptions.

**1. Employment Support**

Governments should invest in workforce development, retraining programmes and targeted employment support to improve labour market resilience.

**2. Effective Monetary Policies**

Central banks should balance inflation control with economic growth, avoiding unnecessary disruptions caused by abrupt monetary policy changes.

**3. Supply Chain Diversification**

Countries should reduce excessive dependence on individual suppliers, strengthen regional trade relationships and invest in domestic production capabilities.

**4. Inflation Management**

Policymakers should address supply-side pressures, improve domestic production and implement targeted measures to protect consumers from rising living costs.

**5. Digital Economy Investment**

Investment in digital infrastructure, artificial intelligence, automation and renewable energy can support productivity, innovation and long-term economic development.

**6. Financial Market Stability**

Improved financial transparency, risk monitoring and long-term investment strategies can strengthen investor confidence and reduce financial market instability.

## Conclusion

The COVID-19 pandemic created significant economic challenges across the world, affecting employment, GDP growth, inflation, manufacturing, services and financial markets.

However, the recovery experience varied considerably between economies. Some countries demonstrated stronger employment and GDP recovery, while others continued to experience inflationary pressures and slower economic growth.

This project demonstrates how macroeconomic indicators and financial market data can be used to compare economic performance, identify recovery patterns and generate insights that may support economic decision-making.
